What we are discovering today

Central Idea
"Fractions can take different forms without changing their fundamental value."
Think: Why does cutting a cake into more slices not give us more cake?

By the end of this module, you will be able to:

  • Define & Identify equivalent fractions using visual models and numbers.
  • Generate equivalent fractions using the multiplication and division rule.
  • Simplify fractions to their lowest terms using the Highest Common Factor (HCF).
  • Apply equivalent fractions to solve real-world IB inquiry problems.

4. Comprehensive Material & Method Workbench

Master the Golden Rules of Equivalent Fractions

Method 1: Multiplication Rule

Generating Equivalent Fractions

To find an equivalent fraction, multiply both the top number (numerator) and the bottom number (denominator) by the same non-zero number ($n$).

ab = a × nb × n

📝 Worked Example:

Start with 23:

  • • Multiply by 2 → 2 × 23 × 2 = 46
  • • Multiply by 5 → 2 × 53 × 5 = 1015
  • • Multiply by 10 → 2 × 103 × 10 = 2030
Method 2: Division Rule

Simplifying Fractions (Lowest Terms)

To simplify a fraction to its lowest terms, divide both the numerator and denominator by their Highest Common Factor (HCF).

ab = a ÷ HCFb ÷ HCF

📝 Worked Example:

Simplify 1218:

  • Factors of 12: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12
  • Factors of 18: 1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18
  • Divide both by HCF = 6 → 23
